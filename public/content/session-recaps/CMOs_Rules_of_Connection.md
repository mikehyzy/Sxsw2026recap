# How CMOs Rewrote the Rules of Connection at SXSW 2026

## Overview

The Lyft and Samsung CMOs took the stage at SXSW 2026 to deliver one of the conference's most substantive marketing sessions — arguing that AI doesn't kill authenticity but supercharges it. "Featured Session: How CMOs Are Rewriting the Rules of Connection," held on March 18, 2026, featured Brian Irving (CMO, Lyft) and Allison Stransky (CMO, Samsung Electronics America), with Marty Swant of Fast Company serving as moderator and Eleanor Hawkins of Axios participating as a panelist.

The session crystallized a broader shift at SXSW 2026: the marketing industry has moved past debating whether to adopt AI and is now grappling with how to build organizations capable of deploying it responsibly. Both CMOs offered granular operational detail — from Samsung's cross-functional "AI crews" to Lyft's use of Google's Gemini agents — making this far more than a typical "AI will save us all" panel.

## The Session That Almost Had a Different Lineup

The full official title was "Featured Session: How CMOs Are Rewriting the Rules of Connection," placed in SXSW's Brand & Marketing track and scheduled for Wednesday, March 18, 2026, from 1:00–2:00 PM CT at the Hilton Austin Downtown, Hilton Grand Ballroom (Salon HJK).

The official SXSW schedule listed it under event code PP1148491. The session description stated:

> "Modern CMOs have cracked the code: AI doesn't kill authenticity — it supercharges it. In this session, several CMOs skip the hype to share what actually works. They've turned algorithms into empathy and data into deeper human connections. No fluff, no buzzwords — just the real wins, messy learnings, and breakthrough moments that separate leaders from followers."

Notably, the panel underwent a significant lineup change between its October 2025 announcement and the actual event. SXSW's original press release listed three CMOs: Brian Irving (Lyft), Allison Stransky (Samsung), and Lauren Stafford Webb (SoFi) — with Eleanor Hawkins of Axios serving as moderator. By the time the session took place, Stafford Webb had dropped off the panel, Hawkins had shifted from moderator to panelist, and Marty Swant of Fast Company stepped in as moderator. Capitol Communicator's on-site coverage from March 19, 2026, confirmed Swant's role with photos showing him leading the discussion alongside Stransky and Irving.

## Brian Irving Brought Lyft's AI-Native Playbook to the Stage

Brian Irving has served as Chief Marketing Officer of Lyft since March 20, 2024, leading all marketing, creative, communications, and brand strategy. He arrived at Lyft with one of the most varied résumés in modern marketing.

After studying international relations at Michigan State University, Irving began as a financial analyst before pivoting to account management at agencies Digitas and RAPP. He then held marketing roles at Apple (Global Brand Director for Google+ and Senior Director of Marketing for Google Play), at Airbnb as Global Marketing Director (where he launched the celebrated "Live There" campaign repositioning the brand around local experiences), at Hampton Creek as Co-CMO and VP of Global Digital Marketing, at Levi Strauss & Co. as Chief Brand/Marketing Officer during its IPO, and at Eventbrite during its IPO, creating the "Made for Those Who Do" brand campaign.

From 2020 to 2021, Irving led marketing for Meta Reality Labs as CMO and VP, overseeing launches of Meta Quest Pro, Ray-Ban smart glasses, and the first-ever Meta × NBA partnership. Outside the office, Irving serves on the board of The Trevor Project, the leading LGBTQIA+ youth suicide prevention nonprofit, and is openly gay. He grew up in Flint, Michigan, and lives in San Francisco with his son.

At SXSW, Irving described Lyft's approach to AI as building an "AI-native organization rooted in psychological safety." He revealed that Lyft uses Google's Gemini to build agents that synthesize meeting notes, documents, and campaign materials into go-to-market strategies. His most quotable line captured the investment reality:

> "For every dollar we spend on new AI tools, we need two to three dollars to invest in training."

He pushed back hard against peers cutting agency relationships, stating:

> "I have peers who are like, 'I'm getting rid of my agency because of AI.' I think that's wrong. You want external perspective on innovation and creativity."

His overarching philosophy: "It's not human versus machine. It is human and machine."

## Allison Stransky Revealed Samsung's Enterprise-Wide AI Transformation

Allison Stransky is Chief Marketing Officer and VP of Corporate Marketing at Samsung Electronics America, where she spearheads the company's "One Samsung: Better Together and Enhanced by AI" strategy. Her career spans 12+ years across Johnson & Johnson, Unilever, and L'Oréal.

Before joining Samsung, she led global strategy for the Creative Works team at Google, where she worked on the Creative Works team. She holds a BS from Bucknell University and a Master's in Cosmetics and Fragrance Marketing from the Fashion Institute of Technology.

She has been recognized as Digiday's Marketing Executive of the Year and won Advertising Week's Future Is Female Award. She also serves on the board of Helen Keller Services.

Stransky's SXSW remarks revealed the structural depth of Samsung's AI commitment. She described a cross-functional AI task force that pulls in marketing, legal, HR, and customer service, noting that "every function has what we call an AI crew" — a deliberate structure designed to break down silos and accelerate adoption.

This begins at the CEO level, where Samsung's DX Division Head TM Roh introduced "AX" (AI Transformation) as a company reorganization principle. Stransky emphasized a test-and-learn mindset: Samsung pilots tools and runs controlled experiments rather than committing to a single platform.

Perhaps her most forward-looking insight concerned Generative Engine Optimization (GEO). She declared:

> "SEO is not over. But here comes GEO."

She explained that PR is more important than ever, adding that brands need real, credible information all over the internet. She also noted that employees using AI "aren't necessarily working less — they're actually putting in more hours. They're energized by AI, and time saved is redeployed elsewhere."

## Marty Swant and Eleanor Hawkins Shaped the Conversation from Journalism's Vantage

Marty Swant is a freelance journalist based in Brooklyn, New York, currently contributing to Fast Company, The New York Times, Inc., Big Technology (Alex Kantrowitz's newsletter), Adweek, and VentureBeat. His career arc traces from the University of Missouri School of Journalism through the Associated Press (covering Alabama politics), Adweek (covering emerging tech and marketing, ~2015–2019), Forbes (editing the CMO Network and managing the World's Most Valuable Brands and CMO Next lists, ~2019–2022), Digiday (senior reporter, ~2022–2025), before going freelance in May 2025.

His extensive panel moderation experience — spanning Advertising Week, Digiday's Media Buying Summit, Braze Forge, and now SXSW — made him a natural fit to step in as moderator. He covered SXSW 2026 extensively for Fast Company, writing on topics from deepfake installations to AI documentaries.

Eleanor Hawkins is a Communications Strategist and Writer at Axios, where she authors the weekly Axios Communicators newsletter covering trends that impact how leaders, brands, and employers communicate. She also helped launch Axios Communicators Pro, a premium membership community for PR professionals. Unlike the other panelists, Hawkins came from a communications practitioner background rather than traditional journalism — Axios specifically recruited her because they "wanted to find somebody with a communication background" who understood the nuance of corporate communications.

She had identified AI as reshaping communications through content creation, message testing, personalization, and discoverability in a December 2024 McKinsey interview. At SXSW 2026, she also co-hosted "Hit Pause at SXSW with Yahoo x Axios Communicators" on March 15.

## Samsung and Lyft Are Deploying AI Across Fundamentally Different Use Cases

Samsung's AI marketing operates at enormous scale. The company aims to double Galaxy AI-enabled devices to 800 million and declared 2026 an "AI-first" year. Recent campaigns include "Why Samsung" (a 50-country global push highlighting Bespoke AI and SmartThings), "Your Home Speaks You" (using data signals to create persona-driven personalized paths), and Galaxy S25/S26 AI launch campaigns with BBH Singapore.

On the advertising side, Samsung deployed Smartly Conversational Ads for the Galaxy Flip7/Fold7 launch, achieving a 73% lower CPA and 3x return on ad spend. Samsung Ads itself launched an AI-enhanced advertising suite with ML-driven targeting using 250+ mobile-based segments and proprietary contextual alignment. The company is also investing in Reddit specifically because LLMs surface Reddit content, creating compounding visibility effects.

Lyft's AI deployment focuses on operational speed and consumer experience. Its partnership with Anthropic (using Claude via Amazon Bedrock) yielded an 87% reduction in average customer service resolution time, with more than half of support requests handled in under three minutes.

The proprietary Symphony marketing automation platform takes business objectives, predicts future user value, allocates budget, and publishes across channels autonomously. For drivers, the AI-powered Earnings Assistant (launched April 2025) uses predictive models to suggest optimal driving times and locations based on traffic, demand, and local events. Irving described Lyft as a "real-time personalization engine" operating across 300+ micro-marketplaces.

Lyft Media, the company's advertising platform, reaches riders during an average 24-minute ride with 76.78% on-target ad delivery — well above industry norms.

## SXSW 2026 Made AI's Organizational Implications the Main Event

The CMO panel fit squarely within SXSW 2026's dominant narrative. In its 40th anniversary year (March 12–18), the festival ran all programming — Innovation, Film & TV, and Music conferences — concurrently for the first time across seven days. Nearly one-third of PanelPicker proposals touched on AI, making it the most popular topic.

Key tensions ran throughout the programming: AI versus human authenticity, the "bland tax" that LLMs impose on generic content, and the fundamental question of who controls discovery, customer relationships, and content value when AI mediates.

The CMO session embodied these tensions precisely. Both Irving and Stransky refused to frame AI as a replacement for human creativity or agency partnerships. Instead, they presented a pragmatic middle path: AI as operational infrastructure requiring massive training investment, structural reorganization (Samsung's AI crews, Lyft's 3:1 training-to-tools ratio), and a philosophical commitment that technology serves people, not metrics.

Their shared insistence that PR and agencies matter "more than ever" in an AI-mediated world pushed back against the prevailing narrative of AI-driven disintermediation — and may prove to be the session's most consequential argument.

## Conclusion

This session delivered unusual substance for a conference panel. The most important takeaway was not about AI's capabilities but about its organizational demands. Samsung's AI crew structure and Lyft's 3:1 training-to-tools investment ratio represent concrete frameworks other marketing organizations can adapt.

Stransky's identification of GEO as the successor to SEO signals a genuine strategic shift that will reshape how brands approach discoverability. Irving's defense of agency relationships challenges the cost-cutting zeitgeist with a compelling logic: that external perspective becomes more, not less, valuable when internal teams gain AI-powered efficiency.

The lineup change (Swant replacing Hawkins as moderator, Stafford Webb departing) suggests the session was in flux, but the final configuration — two operational CMOs guided by a seasoned tech-marketing journalist with a communications practitioner in the room — produced a more focused dialogue than a three-CMO format might have. For marketing leaders tracking AI's real-world implementation, this panel offered the rarest commodity at SXSW: specificity.
