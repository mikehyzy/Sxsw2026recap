# How Crypto Is Building Capital Markets for Everyone: The Full SXSW 2026 Session Deep Dive

## Overview

Crypto's boldest case at SXSW 2026 came from two insiders — the Solana Foundation's consumer chief and a top Silicon Valley VC — who argued that prediction markets, branded stablecoins, and AI-powered agents are dismantling Wall Street's monopoly on capital formation. The session, titled "How Crypto Is Building New Capital Markets for Everyone, not just Wall Street," ran as a Featured Session on Saturday, March 14, at 4:00–5:00 PM CT. It was one of only a handful of crypto-focused sessions in a conference dominated by AI programming, yet it drew pre-event praise from Austin's startup ecosystem as "the most current articulation of where blockchain and AI are converging in terms of capital formation."

## Prediction Markets Explode Beyond Politics Into a $63 Billion Industry

The prediction markets landscape discussed at the session has transformed dramatically. Combined trading volume across all prediction market platforms reached approximately $63.5 billion in 2025 — a fourfold increase from roughly $9 billion in 2024. The two dominant platforms, Polymarket and Kalshi, combined for about $40 billion of that total.

Polymarket defied post-election decline predictions after its $3.3 billion presidential election moment. The platform processed a record $7 billion in February 2026 alone, shattering its Election Day record with $425 million in a single day on February 28. Active traders surpassed 450,000.

After the DOJ and CFTC closed investigations in July 2025, Polymarket acquired CFTC-licensed exchange ODEX. In December 2025, it received CFTC no-action relief and soft-launched "Polymarket US" with KYC requirements — starting with sports markets. ICE (the NYSE parent) invested up to $2 billion at a $9 billion valuation; secondary market implied valuation reached $11.6 billion by January 2026.

Strategic partnerships now include Dow Jones (real-time data across WSJ, MarketWatch, Barron's) and Major League Baseball (exclusive prediction markets partner, signed March 19, 2026).

Kalshi, the first CFTC-approved Designated Contract Market, captured over 60% of global prediction market share with $50 billion in annualized 2025 volume — up from $300 million in 2024. Revenue trajectory tells the story: $1.8 million (2023) to $24 million (2024) to an estimated $260 million (2025). Kalshi raised $300 million Series D led by Sequoia, a16z, and Paradigm at a $5 billion valuation in October 2025, then a $1 billion round at $11 billion valuation in December.

Sports dominate (~90% of volume), with the Super Bowl 2026 alone generating $1 billion in contracts.

The session's mention of travel and community betting reflects nascent but emerging applications. Kalshi already offers weather and transportation-related contracts. Manifold Markets operates as the closest model to community betting — a play-money platform where anyone can create a market on any topic. Kalshi CEO Mansour also spoke at SXSW 2026 on creating prediction markets for cultural events.

New entrants flooding the market include DraftKings Predictions (acquired a DCM), FanDuel Predicts (partnered with CME Group), Fanatics Markets, Robinhood (2.3 billion contracts traded Q3 2025 via Kalshi integration), and Crypto.com's OG app.

## Branded Stablecoins and the $300 Billion Stablecoin Revolution

The stablecoin market crossed $300 billion in market capitalization in early 2026, with 232+ million holders globally and transfer volume rising 72% in 2025 to $33 trillion. The GENIUS Act — signed into law July 18, 2025 as the first federal stablecoin legislation — created a comprehensive regulatory framework requiring 1:1 reserve backing, monthly audits, and Bank Secrecy Act compliance, while explicitly declaring stablecoins are neither securities nor commodities. The act takes effect January 2027.

The session's reference to "brand-specific cryptocurrencies" describes a rapidly materializing trend: branded stablecoins issued by corporations for their own ecosystems.

- Stripe/Bridge launched Open Issuance, allowing any business to create its own stablecoin with interoperable swaps
- Coinbase launched Custom Stablecoins — a stablecoin-as-a-service product letting businesses create branded tokens backed 1:1 by USDC
- Fiserv launched FIUSD, integrated into Mastercard's network reaching 150+ million merchants
- PayPal's PYUSD ($1.54 billion market cap) expanded with a PYUSDx developer framework in February 2026
- Amazon and Walmart are both exploring issuing their own digital dollars
- Revolut is building infrastructure for a USD-pegged token across its 30+ million users
- Phantom wallet launched "CASH" on Stripe's platform for its 15 million users

For small businesses and creators, stablecoins solve real friction. 81% of crypto-aware small businesses expressed interest in using stablecoins in a 2025 survey. Visa is explicitly targeting 30 million creators globally with stablecoin payments, noting that "there are 30 million creators in markets where the local currency isn't really strong — that's where stablecoins can offer a sweet spot."

Platforms like Zora (1.5 million creator tokens, $420 million total trading volume, 50% of fees redistributed to creators) demonstrate how tokenization enables direct creator monetization without intermediaries.

## AI Agents Need Crypto Wallets Because They Can't Open Bank Accounts

The AI-crypto integration discussed in the session reflects one of the fastest-moving developments in both fields. The core thesis is straightforward: AI agents cannot satisfy KYC requirements to open bank accounts, but they can generate a crypto wallet with just a private key — making blockchain the natural financial infrastructure for autonomous agents.

Coinbase launched Agentic Wallets in February 2026 — the first wallet infrastructure designed specifically for AI agents to independently hold funds, send payments, trade tokens, and earn yield. This runs on the x402 protocol (named after HTTP's "Payment Required" status code), which has processed 50 million transactions since launch and embeds stablecoin payments directly into HTTP requests.

The x402 Foundation, co-launched with Cloudflare in September 2025, counts AWS among its backers. The economics are compelling: Stripe's minimum processing fee of ~$0.30 per transaction makes AI micro-tasks (sub-cent payments) prohibitively expensive on card rails, while on-chain via x402, total cost drops below $0.02.

Visa Crypto Labs launched an experimental CLI on March 18, 2026, enabling bots and AI workflows to make programmatic card payments. Google introduced Agent Payment Protocol 2.0 in January 2025 with x402 as its settlement layer. BNB Chain deployed autonomous agent payment infrastructure in February 2026.

The broader AI-crypto ecosystem includes infrastructure tokens like Bittensor (TAO) for decentralized AI model networks, Fetch.ai (FET) for autonomous agent economies, Render (RNDR) for decentralized GPU computing, and Virtuals Protocol for tokenized AI agent creation.

The DeF AI (Decentralized Finance + AI) category encompasses agents that monitor hundreds of DeFi protocols simultaneously, automatically moving funds to optimal yields. As a16z crypto noted in its 2026 trends report, "the bottleneck for the agent economy is shifting from intelligence to identity" — with non-human identities outnumbering human employees 96-to-1 in financial services while remaining "unbanked ghosts."

## The Regulatory Sea Change That Brought Crypto Founders Home

The backdrop to everything discussed in this session is the fundamental reversal of U.S. crypto policy under the Trump administration. On January 23, 2025 — three days after inauguration — an executive order established the policy of "strengthening American leadership in digital financial technology," created the President's Working Group on Digital Asset Markets (chaired by AI and Crypto Czar David Sacks), and revoked all Biden-era crypto directives.

The enforcement shift was immediate and sweeping. Approximately 89 cryptocurrency enforcement cases were dropped or frozen, including the SEC's lawsuits against Coinbase (dismissed February 27, 2025 with prejudice, zero penalties), Ripple (settlement finalized August 2025, penalty reduced from $2 billion demand to $50 million), and Kraken (dismissed March 2025). The DOJ disbanded its National Cryptocurrency Enforcement Team in April 2025.

SEC Chairman Paul Atkins launched "Project Crypto" in July 2025, proposing a token taxonomy with four categories — digital commodities, digital collectibles, digital tools (none of which would be securities), and tokenized securities (which would be). The vision includes a "super-app" registration enabling a single license for broker-dealers to offer trading, staking, lending, and custody.

The SEC approved generic listing standards reducing crypto ETF approval from 270 days to a 75-day maximum, and over 90 crypto ETF applications are active, including filings for Solana, XRP, Cardano, and Dogecoin.

A Strategic Bitcoin Reserve was established by executive order on March 6, 2025, funded by roughly 207,000 seized Bitcoin (~$17 billion at the time). The GENIUS Act became law in July 2025. The CLARITY Act — successor to FIT21, establishing CFTC jurisdiction over digital commodities and SEC authority over crypto securities — passed the House 294-134 in July 2025 and awaits Senate reconciliation, with Treasury Secretary Bessent targeting "spring 2026."

VC investment in U.S. crypto reached $7.9 billion in 2025, up 44% year-over-year, capturing 42% of global crypto VC funding. The OCC granted national trust bank charters to BitGo, Circle, Fidelity Digital Assets, Paxos, and Ripple. SoFi became the first U.S. chartered bank offering direct digital asset trading. Traditional giants — JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, PNC — are all developing crypto products.

## Conclusion: Capital Formation Is Being Rewritten in Real Time

This session captured a pivotal moment. Miranda and Gonzalez represent two sides of the same coin: the builder (Solana Foundation, constructing consumer crypto infrastructure) and the funder (Foundation Capital, deploying $600 million into companies using it).

Their thesis — that crypto is democratizing capital markets beyond Wall Street — is supported by concrete developments: prediction markets generating $63.5 billion in volume, stablecoins crossing $300 billion market cap with the first federal legislation in place, branded corporate stablecoins launching via Stripe and Coinbase, and AI agents requiring crypto wallets because traditional finance cannot serve them.

The most important insight is that these trends are converging. Branded stablecoins give small businesses programmable money. Prediction markets let communities price any event. AI agents need crypto rails for micro-transactions. And the regulatory clarity from the Trump administration has created conditions where building in U.S. is no longer a compliance liability but a competitive advantage.

For business leaders whose operations involve cross-border payments, treasury management, or any commercial process currently dependent on legacy payment rails, the infrastructure Circle described at this session is already in testnet and approaching production readiness.
