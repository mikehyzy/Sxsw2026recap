# Circle's CTO Made the Case for Programmable Money at SXSW 2026

## Overview

Li Fan, Chief Technology and AI Officer at Circle, took the SXSW 2026 stage to argue that the convergence of AI and blockchain is not a speculative thesis but an infrastructure buildout already underway. In conversation with Mallun Yen, founder and CEO of Operator Collective, Fan presented Circle's position at the intersection of two parallel networks: the Internet of Intelligence, where autonomous AI agents reason and act, and the Internet of Value, where tokenized assets and stablecoins move across borders without friction.

The session moved between the philosophical framing of that convergence and the operational specifics of Circle's own AI transformation, including the lessons Fan drew from leading a heavily regulated fintech company through rapid AI adoption.

## The Session and Its Official Listing

The full official title was "Featured Session: The Internet of Value Meets the Internet of Intelligence," part of the second wave of Featured Sessions announced for SXSW 2026's Innovation Conference in December 2025. The SXSW app truncated it to "FEATURED SESSION: THE INTERNET OF VALUE ME..."

The session was accessible to Platinum and Innovation badge holders. Two speakers were listed: Li Fan (Chief Technology and AI Officer, Circle) and Mallun Yen (CEO and Founder, Operator Collective). Fan confirmed on LinkedIn following the session that it was a pleasure to share the stage with Yen to discuss the real-world challenges of enterprise AI transformation and the intersection of stablecoins and AI, indicating Yen functioned as the interviewer.

The official SXSW session description stated:

> "As AI and blockchain mature in parallel, we are witnessing the emergence of two powerful networks: the Internet of Intelligence, where autonomous AI systems reason and act, and the Internet of Value, where tokenized assets and stablecoins move frictionlessly across borders. Li Fan, Circle's Chief Technology and AI Officer, will discuss her vision for building compliant and scalable financial infrastructure to support the convergence of AI and blockchain."

## Li Fan and the Background She Brought to the Stage

Li Fan grew up in Shanghai, China, where she was encouraged toward science and engineering despite an early interest in design and architecture. She studied computer science at the University of Wisconsin-Madison. Her career began at Cisco Systems as a software engineer before she moved into research and product development roles that established her as an expert in visual computation and computer vision.

She was named one of Forbes' America's Top 50 Women in Tech in 2018 and identified by Business Insider as one of the most important engineers in the world.

Fan's executive career spanned four of the most technically demanding consumer and enterprise platforms of the past two decades. At Baidu, China's dominant search engine, she served as Vice President of Engineering. At Google, she was Senior Director of Engineering. At Pinterest, she served as SVP of Engineering, leading the platform through significant infrastructure scaling. At Lime, the micro-mobility company, she served as both CTO and interim CPO.

She joined Circle in 2021 as CTO, and her role expanded to Chief Technology and AI Officer as Circle moved aggressively into AI transformation.

Circle is the issuer of USDC, the second-largest stablecoin globally by market capitalization, fully backed by dollar reserves. The company completed its IPO in 2026 and operates as regulated financial infrastructure serving major banks, asset managers, payment networks, and cryptocurrency exchanges globally, including partnerships with Visa, BlackRock, Goldman Sachs, BNY Mellon.

Circle's Arc blockchain, which launched its public testnet shortly before SXSW 2026, is designed as an economic operating system combining sub-second finality with AI-ready architecture and stablecoins as gas — representing a concrete technological expression of the Internet of Value and Internet of Intelligence convergence.

## Mallun Yen and Her Role in the Session

Mallun Yen founded Operator Collective in 2019 as a venture capital fund and community built around the thesis that the most valuable capital in early-stage investing is operational expertise from people who have built and scaled large technology companies. The fund's limited partners, numbering more than 150, are drawn predominantly from operating roles at companies including Salesforce, Stripe, Zoom, Cloudflare, and Confluent. The fund is 90 percent women and 40 percent people of color among its LPs, and 70 percent of LPs had not previously invested in venture before joining.

Yen's earlier career included serving as Vice President of Worldwide Intellectual Property at Cisco and building RPX Corporation, a patent risk management company, from zero to $100 million in revenue and through its IPO under Yen's leadership. She is also a co-founder of SaaStr, the world's largest B2B software community, and ChIPsNetwork.org, a global community for women in technology, law, and policy she co-founded in 2005.

She serves as a board director of Pure Storage. Her background as an IP attorney, operator, and investor made her a credible interlocutor for understanding both the regulatory and infrastructure dimensions of Circle's business.

## The Convergence Argument Fan Made at the Session

Fan's central argument was that AI agents and programmable money are converging toward a single infrastructure layer that will reshape how commerce functions. In the current model, AI agents operating autonomously on behalf of users encounter a friction point every time a financial transaction is required: the agent must hand off to a human or interface with payment systems designed for human interaction.

Programmable money, specifically stablecoins operating on blockchain infrastructure, eliminates that friction by allowing AI agents to transact directly, instantly, and globally without the latency, cost, and regulatory complexity of legacy payment rails.

Fan described Circle's Open Claw hackathon as a demonstration environment for this convergence, where teams experimented with AI agents using stablecoins to execute transactions autonomously. The controlled testnet environment allowed participants to stress-test the infrastructure before any real capital was at risk. Fan framed this experimental approach — test and learn within bounded environments before committing to production — as the responsible methodology for any organization building at the intersection of AI and financial services.

## On AI Agents in Commerce: Accountability Without Automation of Judgment

On the question of AI agents in commerce, Fan acknowledged that autonomous AI execution of financial decisions creates genuine accountability questions. Her position was that human judgment remains indispensable at the design and governance layer even as execution becomes automated: the people building these systems must remain responsible for the rules agents follow, the limits placed on their autonomy, and the escalation paths when edge cases arise.

She argued this is not a technical problem but an organizational and ethical one, and one that compliance-oriented companies like Circle are better positioned to solve than pure technology companies without regulatory experience. Fan positioned broad organizational AI literacy as a competitive requirement rather than an optional investment, particularly for companies whose product itself is financial infrastructure that must meet regulatory standards.

## Circle's AI Transformation and Fan's Operational Lessons

Fan drew on Circle's internal AI transformation as a case study for what responsible AI adoption looks like inside a publicly traded, heavily regulated fintech company of approximately 1,000 employees.

Her most quoted observation addressed a common misconception about AI-driven efficiency: that AI-generated code does not automatically translate into equivalent workforce reduction. The relationship between AI tooling and organizational output is not linear. Time saved in one area tends to be redeployed into higher-order tasks rather than eliminated, and engineering teams empowered by AI tools tend to tackle more ambitious problems rather than simply produce the same work faster.

She also argued that effective AI adoption requires the entire organization to understand the core problem being solved, not just engineers. When non-technical team members lack sufficient context about what a system is trying to accomplish, AI tools produce outputs that are technically functional but strategically misaligned. Fan positioned broad organizational AI literacy as a competitive requirement rather than an optional investment, particularly for companies whose product itself is financial infrastructure that must meet regulatory standards.

## Conclusion

This session occupied a specific and underserved position in SXSW 2026's AI programming: it addressed AI not from the perspective of marketing, creativity, or workforce strategy, but from the perspective of financial infrastructure. Fan's argument that programmable money and AI agents are converging into a single execution layer for commerce is among the most consequential structural claims made at SXSW 2026, because it describes a change not in how companies use technology internally but in how economic activity itself will be organized.

For business leaders whose operations involve cross-border payments, treasury management, or any commercial process currently dependent on legacy payment rails, the infrastructure Circle described at this session is already in testnet and approaching production readiness.
