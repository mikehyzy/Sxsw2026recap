**Presenters:** Serena Williams (Entrepreneur-in-Residence, Reckitt Catalyst), Ryan Dullea (Chief Category Growth Officer, Reckitt), Catherine Casey Nanda (Co-Founder & Managing Partner, Acumen America), Kwamane Liddell (Founder, ThriveLink), Mika Eddy (Co-Founder, Malama Health)

## Overview

Serena Williams took the stage not as a celebrity endorser but as **Entrepreneur-in-Residence for Reckitt Catalyst**, actively involved in selecting, mentoring, and supporting health-focused founders from underrepresented backgrounds. The session paired Williams's star power with a panel of four founders solving specific health access crises—from maternal mortality to digital healthcare divides—and demonstrated that the entrepreneurs best positioned to solve America's healthcare challenges are those with direct, lived experience of the problems themselves.

This session occupied a distinctive position in SXSW 2026's landscape: it was the concrete, specific instantiation of the broader themes about human-centered innovation that animated the festival. Where Amy Webb mapped macro convergences and Sandy Carter outlined AI implementation, Williams and Reckitt Catalyst showed what human-centered health innovation looks like when deployed at the community level.

## Panel: Who Was on Stage and Why

### Serena Williams — Entrepreneur-in-Residence, Reckitt Catalyst

Williams's role here is not merely celebrity endorsement. She serves as **Entrepreneur-in-Residence**, actively involved in selecting cohort members and mentoring founders. Her connection to health equity is personal and professional:

**Personal connection:** She has spoken publicly about her own near-fatal pregnancy complications (emergency C-section, pulmonary embolism) and the disproportionate maternal mortality crisis among Black women in America—the exact crisis Mika Eddy's Malama Health addresses.

**Professional operation:** Williams runs **Serena Ventures**, a venture capital firm she founded in 2014 that has invested in over 100 companies with a portfolio heavily weighted toward diverse founders. The firm's investments span fintech (Ellevest), wellness (Koia), education (MasterClass), and consumer goods (Paddle8, Canva).

**Strategic insight:** At SXSW 2026, in an Axios interview (same day), Williams revealed that **one of the toughest hurdles for early-stage entrepreneurs is learning to sell themselves**. She noted that founders—especially underrepresented ones—with the best products often fail to raise money because they can't get that idea across to other people to believe in them. She shared that her own mentor told her she needed to become a better storyteller, and that advice transformed her business trajectory.

This insight directly connects to the session's focus: the gatekeeping mechanism in venture capital often isn't product quality but founder narrative fit, and underrepresented founders are at a structural disadvantage in translating their lived expertise into investor-compelling narratives. Williams's presence and mentorship is partly about helping founders overcome that narrative gap.

### Ryan Dullea — Chief Category Growth Officer, Reckitt

Dullea represents the corporate side of the partnership. Reckitt (formerly Reckitt Benckiser) is a British multinational consumer goods company behind brands including Enfamil, Lysol, Mucinex, Clearasil, Durex, Woolite, and Finish—with approximately $16 billion in annual revenue.

Dullea articulated Reckitt's macro framing: **roughly 1 in 3 Americans have limited or no access to essential health services such as hospitals, pharmacies and primary care providers.** This positioned the session as addressing a market opportunity: companies that solve this access crisis are solving for a massive underserved population.

His role was to articulate why a global CPG company is investing in early-stage health entrepreneurs—the business case for mission-driven innovation.

### Catherine Casey Nanda — Co-Founder & Managing Partner, Acumen America

Nanda represents the investment infrastructure. **Acumen America is the leading venture capital firm investing in mission-driven entrepreneurs looking to fix the root causes of America's poverty crisis.** It is the U.S. arm of Acumen, the global impact investing fund founded by Jacqueline Novogratz.

Nanda's role in Reckitt Catalyst is to manage selection, due diligence, and strategic guidance for American Catalyst cohort members. Her key quote from the session announcement: **"This partnership is about shifting power and resources toward the people best positioned to solve America's greatest health challenges. Women and underrepresented founders are often closest to the backbones of the healthcare workforce but remain significantly underfunded. By joining forces with Reckitt and Serena Williams, we are providing the strategic guidance and capital necessary to turn these bold ideas into lasting, system-changing companies that can improve health equity across America."**

### Kwamane Liddell — Founder, ThriveLink

Liddell is one of the first two U.S. Catalyst founders unveiled at SXSW. **ThriveLink builds telephonic AI tools that help people with literacy, technology, or access barriers navigate healthcare and social services.** The product reduces administrative burden by unlocking resources for populations that cannot effectively use smartphone apps or web portals—addressing the "digital divide" in healthcare access through voice-first AI.

This positions ThriveLink at the intersection of SXSW's AI track and Health track, demonstrating AI deployed not for tech-literate consumers but for those most excluded by digital-first systems.

### Mika Eddy — Co-Founder, Malama Health

Eddy is the second unveiled U.S. Catalyst founder. **Malama Health provides doula-led, AI-supported maternal care for families insured through Medicaid, improving pregnancy and postpartum outcomes through continuous support from doulas, clinicians, and digital tools.**

The venture directly addresses the U.S. maternal health crisis: Black women in America are 3–4 times more likely to die from pregnancy-related complications than white women, a disparity that persists across income levels. Malama Health's model integrates human care (doulas) with AI monitoring, targeting the Medicaid population that represents roughly 42% of all U.S. births.

Eddy's background exemplifies the founder profile Williams and Reckitt are seeking: direct lived experience with the problem being solved, deep community relationships, and a solution rooted in understanding the actual needs of the population being served.

## Reckitt Catalyst: The Program Behind the Session

**Reckitt Catalyst is a five-year, up to £10 million commitment** to fund, mentor, and support innovative businesses that solve health and hygiene challenges in underserved communities worldwide. The program specifically targets **women and underrepresented entrepreneurs**.

### Program Structure

The program operates through three regional partnerships:

1. **Yunus Social Innovation** (global, associated with Muhammad Yunus's Grameen movement)
2. **Acumen America** (United States)
3. **Health Innovation Exchange (HIEx)** (international)

### Reach and Ambition

Reckitt aims to support **up to 200 founders over five years across 15+ countries**, with particular emphasis on improving healthcare access and relieving pressure on the overburdened American health system.

The U.S. program launched with an open call to entrepreneurs in 2025, with eligible founders still able to apply at Reckitt.com. Funding is described as **"non-dilutive"**—meaning founders receive capital without giving up equity, a critical distinction for early-stage entrepreneurs from underrepresented backgrounds who often cannot afford the dilution that traditional VC demands.

### Williams's Role

Williams's position as Entrepreneur-in-Residence goes beyond symbolic support. Per the session announcement, **"It's been a privilege to be part of choosing this first cohort and mentoring these founders has already been one of the most inspiring parts of my work with Reckitt Catalyst."**

Her quote continues: **"Their ideas come directly from the challenges they see around them, and that authenticity is what drives real innovation."** The emphasis on authenticity—that founders closest to problems are best positioned to solve them—is the session's core argument.

## What This Session Signaled

### Health Equity as Market Opportunity, Not Charity

The session's framing was notably unsentimental. Rather than positioning health equity as a charitable obligation, Dullea framed it as a market opportunity: 1 in 3 Americans face healthcare access barriers. That's not a social problem; that's a $X billion market gap.

This reframing—moving health equity from the "corporate social responsibility" category to the "growth opportunity" category—has significant implications for capital flow and founder support.

### Storytelling as Infrastructure

Williams's Axios quote about storytelling being the make-or-break factor for founders is the session's most load-bearing insight. Venture capital, Williams argued, is fundamentally about narrative fit—can you convince investors of your vision? Underrepresented founders often have stronger product-market fit than their white, male counterparts but weaker narrative-market fit.

By positioning mentorship in storytelling as central to Reckitt Catalyst's value-add, Williams identified the structural gap that non-dilutive funding and board support can help overcome.

### Non-Dilutive Funding as Power Redistribution

The choice to structure Reckitt Catalyst funding as non-dilutive (no equity required) is itself significant. Traditional VC demands dilution in exchange for capital, which preserves founder agency but costs equity. Non-dilutive funding inverts this—founders keep 100% ownership while gaining capital to develop and scale.

In a venture ecosystem that has historically underinvested in women and underrepresented founders, this structure is a direct challenge to the power dynamics of traditional capital.

### Specificity as Credibility

Both ThriveLink and Malama Health are solving for specific, measurable health crises:

- **ThriveLink**: Helping people who can't use digital interfaces access healthcare and social services through voice-first AI
- **Malama Health**: Reducing maternal mortality disparities for Medicaid-covered pregnancies through doula support + AI monitoring

The specificity is striking. These aren't vague "health innovation" pitches; they're laser-focused on particular populations and particular failure modes. This specificity is what makes them credible and fundable.

## Position Within SXSW 2026's Dominant Narratives

### The Founder Profile Williams Is Championing

Williams is building a thesis: the entrepreneurs best positioned to solve endemic social problems are those with direct, lived experience of those problems. This directly challenges the venture capital ecosystem's historic bias toward white, male, privileged founders whose distance from problems has paradoxically been treated as "objectivity."

Williams's argument—that lived experience is a competitive advantage, not a liability—inverts that bias and suggests capital should flow toward founders whose proximity to problems makes them more likely to identify and solve real needs.

### Connection to Broader SXSW Themes

This session provides the ground truth for Amy Webb's "Autonomous Care" convergence and the festival's broader turn toward human-centered innovation. Where Amy Webb identified macro forces reshaping healthcare, Williams showed what human-centered health innovation looks like when deployed by founders with deep community roots.

It also connects to Bhargava's thesis about human connection: both Malama Health (doulas + AI) and ThriveLink (voice-first, not app-first) are designed around understanding how their target populations actually relate to healthcare systems—not how tech entrepreneurs imagine they should.

### The Non-Dilutive Funding Signal

In a festival where venture capital and founder economics are frequent themes, Reckitt Catalyst's non-dilutive structure signals a different model: capital without founder dilution, mentorship without control, and board support without equity stakes. This is itself a statement about founder agency and power.

## Conclusion

Serena Williams's session succeeded precisely because it offered something rare at SXSW 2026: a framework for how venture capital and corporate resources should be redeployed to prioritize founders whose lived experience makes them more likely to solve real problems.

The session demonstrated that the innovation framework most likely to drive breakthrough change in healthcare isn't "more AI" or "more technology"—it's "more capital directed toward founders closest to the problems being solved."

That insight, grounded in three specific companies and one specific program, may be the most actionable statement about entrepreneurship and innovation that SXSW 2026 delivered.
