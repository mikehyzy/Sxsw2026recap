# Phia Takes the SXSW Main Stage: AI Shopping Meets Founder-Led Community

## Overview

Phoebe Gates and Sophia Kianni — Stanford roommates turned co-CEOs of Phia, a $185 million AI shopping startup — delivered a Featured Session at SXSW 2026 on March 13, moderated by their advisor Joanne Bradford, before roughly 1,500 attendees on the main stage. The session title, "Founder-Led Growth: Turning Audience Signal into AI-Powered Commerce," centered on how the co-founders built an AI-native commerce app by leveraging community feedback as their primary product development signal.

Phia launched in April 2025 and crossed one million users within ten months, positioning itself as "Google Flights for fashion" — an AI agent that compares prices across 40,000+ retail and resale sites. The panel combination — a Gen Z climate activist (Gates), a UN climate adviser turned entrepreneur (Kianni), and a $4 billion PayPal acquisition veteran (Bradford) — made it one of the most symbolically loaded sessions of the festival, sitting squarely at the intersection of agentic commerce, the creator economy, and the question of whether startups can compete with Amazon by building trust rather than scale.

The full official title was "Featured Session: Founder-Led Growth: Turning Audience Signal into AI-Powered Commerce" (Session ID PP1150309). It ran Friday, March 13, 2026, from 10:00 to 11:00 AM CT as part of SXSW Innovation Conference's Featured Session programming, accessible to all badge types.

Thematically, it aligned most closely with the Creator Economy track (sponsored by Yamaha, running March 12–15) and the Brand & Marketing track (sponsored by YouTube, running March 12–15), both of which explore how digital creators, communities, and content shape culture and commerce.

The official SXSW description, released March 4, 2026 as part of the festival's final wave of 11 new Featured Sessions, stated: "Phoebe Gates and Sophia Kianni, cofounders of shopping agent Phia, share their experience building an AI-native commerce app while prioritizing meaningful community engagement to inform product roadmap."

The session shared its livestream day with marquee programming including a conversation with Serena Williams and a keynote by Steven Spielberg.

## Joanne Bradford: The Commerce Veteran Behind the Curtain

Joanne Bradford is the founder of JBrad Advisors, a growth consultancy for "elite founders and executives" specializing in market positioning, go-to-market execution, and strategic partnerships. She serves as a strategic advisor and early investor in Phia — not its CEO or founder, as one might initially assume from the panel configuration.

Bradford's career spans nearly four decades of digital media and commerce leadership. She joined Microsoft in 2001 as Corporate Vice President and Chief Revenue Officer of MSN, growing MSN's advertising revenue from near zero to $2.5 billion. She brokered the Facebook advertising deal that preceded Microsoft's landmark investment in the social network and won the Bill Gates Chairman's Award. She helped form the Hulu consortium.

She subsequently served as SVP of Revenue at Yahoo, Chief Marketing and Revenue Officer at Demand Media, President of the San Francisco Chronicle, Head of Partnerships and Chief Revenue Officer at Pinterest (where she led early monetization), and COO/CMO at SoFi (where she helped launch SoFi Invest, Money, and Mortgage products). After Honey, she was Chief Growth Officer at MNTN (the Connected TV ad-tech company where Ryan Reynolds called her "the real deal"), co-founded Enkasa (a real estate tech startup), and served as Chief Money Officer at Domain Money.

Across her career, she claims involvement in over $20 billion in total value creation. Her board and advisory portfolio includes Kahoot!, Snapcommerce, Brit + Co, wealth.com, and she was a founding advisor to LeanIn.org and a board member of CARE. She appeared on Bloomberg's "Masters in Business" podcast (May 2024) and "PPress Play: The StreetCred Podcast" (March 2025).

Her presence as moderator rather than panelist suggests that Phia's operational team values her back-office credibility — she had been "supporting the team behind [Phia] over the past year on copy, product, and go-to-market strategy," as she shared on LinkedIn — signaling deep organizational involvement beyond the formal speaker designation.

## Phoebe Gates: Building Outside the Shadow of a Famous Name

Phoebe Adele Gates, 23, is the youngest child of Bill Gates and Melinda French Gates. She graduated early from Stanford University in June 2024 with a B.S. in Human Biology after three years, having also trained at the School of American Ballet and Juilliard.

At Stanford, she took a generative AI class focused on entrepreneurship where the Phia concept was born. She and Kianni were randomly assigned as roommates, bonded over shared annoyance of juggling shopping tabs, and interviewed hundreds of students to validate the pain point.

Gates has been emphatic about building independently. No money came from her parents — Melinda French Gates publicly stated she would not fund the venture. Gates told Yahoo Finance in February 2026: "My primary motivation is to build something generational that has no ties to, you know, me, my privilege, or my last name." She added: "I have a chip on my shoulder." When Bill Gates first heard the idea, his reaction was skeptical: "A lot of people have tried" e-commerce, and "there's some big guys in there." He also worried she might ask for money. (She did not.)

Beyond Phia, Gates co-produced "Everybody's Fight: An In Bloom Series" with Karlie Kloss — a five-part documentary on reproductive rights. The final short, "Freedom at Stake," premiered at SXSW 2026 as part of the Texas Short Program, meaning Gates was pulling double duty at the festival. She is a Forbes 30 Under 30 (2025, Retail & Ecommerce), TIME100 Next honoree (2025), and Phia was named a TIME Best Invention of 2025. Her media footprint is extensive: appearances on Call Her Daddy with Alex Cooper, TechCrunch Disrupt 2025, Yahoo Finance, and profiles in The New York Times, Fortune, WWD, Inc., and GeekWire.

## Sophia Kianni: From Youngest UN Climate Adviser to AI Commerce Co-CEO

Sophia Danube Kianni, 24, is an Iranian-American entrepreneur born December 13, 2001, in McLean, Virginia. She graduated from Stanford in 2025 with a degree in Science, Technology & Society. Before Phia, she was best known as the founder of Climate Cardinals, the world's largest youth-led climate nonprofit with 16,000+ volunteers in 80+ countries translating climate information into 105+ languages.

In 2020, at age 18, she was appointed to the UN Secretary-General's Youth Advisory Group on Climate Change, making her the youngest United Nations adviser in U.S. history. Her climate credentials include speaking at COP26, COP27, and COP28 (where she launched the "We Wear Oil" campaign about fossil fuels in fashion), co-chairing Youth4Climate in Milan, serving on the EPA's first-ever National Environmental Youth Advisory Council, and earning recognition as Forbes 30 Under 30 (2023, Social Impact), BBC 100 Women (2023), and a National Geographic Young Explorer.

Her TED Talk on language barriers in climate action has over two million views. Her presence on a commerce panel makes sense through Phia's sustainability angle: the app promotes secondhand shopping alongside new items, claiming 75% price savings and 80% carbon reduction versus buying new. However, Kianni's role is not merely that of a sustainability figurehead — she is a full co-CEO who helped conceive the product, participated in fundraising, hosts "The Burnouts" podcast (142,000+ YouTube subscribers, 600,000+ audio downloads, 200 million+ views across platforms) on Alex Cooper's Unwell Network with guests like Kris Jenner, Sara Blakely, Gary Vaynerchuk, and Whitney Wolfe Herd, appeared as a judge for the SXSW EDU 2026 Student Impact Challenge, and attended the Fast Company Grill during the festival.

## What Phia Actually Is and How It Competes

Phia is an AI-powered shopping agent available as a free iOS app and Chrome/Safari browser extension. The name blends Phoebe and Sophia. Its core mechanic is a "Should I Buy This?" button: when a user browses a product online, Phia scans 40,000+ retail and resale sites and a database of 300 million+ fashion items to tell the user whether the price is typical, high, or fair, surfaces cheaper alternatives (both new and secondhand) from platforms like The RealReal, Poshmark, ThredUp, and StockX, tracks price drops, and estimates resale value.

The founders describe it as "what Google Flights, Kayak, and Booking.com do — but for fashion."

Technically, Phia holds U.S. Patent #12,248,974 B1 (registered March 2025) for a "system and method for recommending resale alternatives for retail items." Named inventors include Gates, Kianni, and Silas Alberti, a Stanford AI PhD student. The system combines natural language processing with advanced computer vision to analyze resale products and claims to operate "at 10x the speed and half the cost compared to off-the-shelf GPTs" through proprietary multimodal agents.

The business model is affiliate-based: Phia earns commissions when users purchase through the platform, free for consumers. Performance claims for brand partners are striking: 13% higher conversion, 30% stronger new customer acquisition, 15% higher average order value, and return rates reduced by more than 50%. The return-rate figure is particularly notable in an industry where U.S. retailers faced $890 billion in returns in 2024, with online return rates at 16.9%.

## Funding and Market Positioning

Funding has come rapidly. Initial capital was $850,000 from Soma Capital, a Stanford social entrepreneurship grant, and angel investors. An $8 million seed round in September 2025 was led by Kleiner Perkins, with celebrity investors including Hailey Bieber, Kris Jenner, Sara Blakely, Sheryl Sandberg, and Michael Rubin. A $35 million Series A closed January 27, 2026, led by Notable Capital (Pulse 2.0, Hans Tung) with Khosla Ventures (Keith Rabois) and returning Kleiner Perkins (Annie Case), valuing the company at approximately $185 million. Total funding stands at roughly $43.85 million. The team is about 20 people in a New York City office near Union Square.

Phia enters a market simultaneously crowded and nascent. Amazon Rufus, the dominant incumbent, serves over 300 million customers with AI-powered shopping assistance, generating an estimated $12 billion in incremental authorized sales in 2025 and integrated into Amazon's full logistics stack. But critics note that 83% of Rufus recommendations are Amazon-sold products and Amazon Basics appears in 41% of results — exactly the bias that startups like Phia pitch against.

ChatGPT Shopping processes 50 million shopping queries daily across 700 million weekly users, though OpenAI pulled its "Instant Checkout" feature in March 2026 due to low conversion — a significant signal that in-chat purchasing may be premature. Perplexity Shopping launched in November 2024 and launched the aggressive Comet browser to let AI agents shop on any website, triggering a lawsuit from Amazon. Google is building the Universal Commerce Protocol with Shopify, Walmart, Target, and payment networks.

The most direct competitor to Phia is likely Daydream, a fashion-focused AI shopping startup founded by Julie Bornstein (ex-Nordstrom, Sephora, Stitch Fix) with $50 million in seed funding from Forerunner Ventures, Index Ventures, and Google Ventures. Daydream takes a 20% commission and works with 2,000+ brand partners but remains in beta. Both Phia and Daydream bet on domain specificity — fashion expertise rather than general-purpose search — as their moat against big tech.

Market projections for agentic commerce are enormous: McKinsey estimates AI agents could mediate $3–5 trillion in global consumer commerce by 2030; Morgan Stanley projects $190–385 billion in U.S. agentic e-commerce spending by 2030. But a PYMNTS analysis published the final day of SXSW 2026 (March 18) argued "Agentic Commerce Isn't Here: AI Shopping Is a Smarter Search Bar," noting the gap between aspiration and reality remains wide.

## Phia's Privacy Crisis and Operational Challenges

A significant blemish emerged in November 2025 when Fortune reported that security researchers discovered Phia's browser extension was capturing full HTML of every webpage users visited — including bank statements and private emails — not just shopping sites. Cybersecurity researcher Charlie Eriksen called it "among some of the crazier things" he had seen. Phia removed the feature and said it "never stored this data," claiming the logging was for "identifying and discovering new retail websites." The company maintains it complies with privacy laws, but the incident raised questions given the extension's deep browser access.

Beyond privacy, the company faces formidable headwinds: Phia's $185 million valuation places it in a precarious position relative to well-capitalized competitors. A November 2025 privacy controversy undermined trust. Daydream has more seasoned e-commerce leadership and three times the funding. Amazon and Google's protocol ambitions could marginalize any single-domain startup. The 50%+ return-rate reduction claim, if durable across scale, represents Phia's most defensible value proposition — but whether that moat holds against Amazon's 300 million customers and Google's protocol ambitions is the open question that will define Phia's next chapter.

## Conclusion

This SXSW session was not merely a product pitch — it was a thesis statement about who gets to build the next era of commerce. Phia's $185 million valuation and one million users demonstrate real traction, but the company faces formidable headwinds: a November 2025 privacy controversy that undermined trust, a competitive field where Daydream has more seasoned e-commerce leadership and three times the funding, and a macro environment where even OpenAI has retreated from in-chat checkout.

What the panel represented concretely was a founder-led, community-informed approach to product development — Phia invites 30 women every two weeks to test and shape product features, pivoted from desktop-first to mobile-first based on community feedback, and treats their podcast audience as a live product development lab. This is founder-led growth in its purest form: personal brand as distribution, audience as R&D, and community as competitive moat.

Whether that moat holds against Amazon's 300 million customers and Google's Universal Commerce Protocol ambitions remains to be seen. But Bradford's presence as moderator — along with her $20 billion value-creation track record — signals that Phia is drawing on the exact playbook that made Honey a $4 billion acquisition target: domain expertise, trust-based positioning, and the ability to reduce friction in how consumers shop.

The combination of Kianni's climate credibility, Gates's media magnetism, and Bradford's operational expertise creates a compelling narrative. Whether it translates into sustainable competitive advantage against vastly larger incumbents is the open question that will define Phia's next chapter — and one the broader e-commerce industry will watch closely as agentic commerce matures from search bar to autonomous system.
