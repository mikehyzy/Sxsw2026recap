# Carl Pei's SXSW 2026 Session: The End of Smartphone Apps

## Overview

Carl Pei, CEO and co-founder of Nothing, used his SXSW 2026 Featured Session to deliver one of the conference's most concrete and commercially grounded predictions about the future of mobile computing. Rather than generalized speculation about AI, Pei presented a specific three-stage framework for how AI agents will make smartphone apps obsolete, backed by $200 million in Series C funding and active product development aimed at that outcome. The session generated same-day coverage from TechCrunch, 9to5Google, Android Central, and at least four other outlets, making it one of the more widely reported individual sessions of SXSW 2026.

## The Session and Its Official Listing

The full official title was "Featured Session: A Conversation with Nothing's CEO and Co-Founder Carl Pei," listed under event code PP1150458 in SXSW's Innovation Conference track. The SXSW app truncated it to "FEATURED SESSION: A CONVERSATION WITH NOT..." cutting off at "Nothing's."

The session took place on Wednesday, March 18, 2026, the final day of SXSW, from 1:00 PM to 2:00 PM CT at the JW Marriott Austin, Salon 6-8. It was announced on February 10, 2026, as part of a new wave of Featured Sessions, with SXSW SVP of Programming Greg Rosenbaum highlighting Pei's "disruptive tech visions" in the announcement. The session was livestreamed on the SXSW YouTube channel, where a recording remains available.

The official session description stated:

> "Join Carl Pei for a wide-ranging conversation about where the tech industry is heading in 2026 and why Nothing is on a mission to make tech for the next generation. In this session, Carl will reflect on how the consumer tech industry became closed and predictable, and why the next era will require a different mindset. He will share his perspective on some of the biggest shifts facing the industry, including the rise of AI-native hardware, and how AI is becoming a core part of the product experience. The conversation will also touch on Nothing's own next chapter, from its continued push into audio to the brand's growing physical footprint with new store openings planned across New York City, Tokyo, London, and India this year."

Two speakers were listed: Carl Pei (CEO and Co-Founder, Nothing) and Nicole Cobler (Reporter, Axios Austin). Cobler functioned as interviewer given the "Conversation with..." format. She co-launched the Axios Austin newsletter in 2021, previously covered technology and state politics for the Austin American-Statesman, and lectures on newsletter writing at the University of Texas at Austin. Unlike the other Featured Sessions in the Innovation track, there were no additional co-panelists.

## Carl Pei and the Background He Brought to the Stage

Carl Pei co-founded OnePlus in 2013, building it into one of the most credible challenger smartphone brands in the Android ecosystem before departing in 2020. He founded Nothing in 2020 with the explicit mission of creating consumer technology that is innovative and fun, a direct response to what he described at SXSW as a smartphone industry that had become closed and predictable.

Nothing's first phone launched in 2022; by 2025 the company had secured a $200 million Series C led by Tiger Global, valuing it as a serious platform contender rather than a niche design brand.

Nothing's product portfolio by the time of SXSW 2026 included the Phone (4a), Phone (4a) Pro, the Nothing Headphone (a) at $199, Nothing OS 4.1, and the Essential Apps platform allowing users to build their own mini apps and widgets. The US was already Nothing's largest market for audio products, and the company had begun carrier partnership discussions ahead of a planned push to take phone sales in the US seriously. Pei's SXSW appearance was therefore not purely a thought-leadership exercise but a commercial positioning move ahead of that expansion.

## Pei's Headline Prediction: "Apps Are Going to Disappear"

The session's most widely reported moment was Pei's direct declaration that apps would disappear. He laid out a three-stage framework for how AI would reshape the smartphone:

**Stage 1** (current phase) involves AI executing single human commands such as booking a flight or reserving a hotel. Pei called this the "super button" and still fundamentally human-in-the-loop.

**Stage 2** (near-term transition) sees AI learning long-term user intentions and proactively surfacing suggestions, from health recommendations to productivity goals. In his framing: when the system knows a user well enough, it will identify things the user did not know they wanted.

**Stage 3** (the destination) is a fully AI-agent-driven phone that acts autonomously, eliminating the need for individual apps entirely.

Pei argued that the smartphone user experience has not fundamentally changed in 20 years: lock screens, home screens, full-screen apps, app stores. He used a practical example to illustrate the inefficiency. Meeting a friend for coffee currently requires four separate apps: messaging, maps, a ride-hailing service, and a calendar. His stated vision for the future:

> "I think the future of smartphones or operating systems should just be: I know you very well, and if I know your intention, I just do it for you."

He estimated the full disappearance of apps would take 7 to 10 years, while acknowledging the smartphone itself would persist for at least five years with a significantly changed operating system.

## AI Agents Need Their Own Interfaces, Not Human Ones

One of Pei's sharpest critiques targeted the approach of having AI agents mimic human touch interactions on phone screens, a reference to Google's Gemini screen automation features. He was unambiguous: that is not the future. His argument was that the future requires purpose-built interfaces for agents, not agents awkwardly operating interfaces designed for human fingers.

He urged developers to open APIs and machine-callable protocols so agents can operate without friction. This critique aligned directly with Nothing's product strategy, which positions Nothing OS as a platform built with AI-native interaction in mind rather than retrofitting AI onto legacy interface paradigms.

This framing also connected to Pei's earlier public claim that the entire phone would eventually have only one app: the operating system itself. At SXSW, he reiterated that position and situated it within the broader competitive context, arguing that founders and startups whose core value resides in an app face disruption whether they plan for it or not.

## Nothing's US Expansion and Retail Ambitions

Beyond the AI predictions, Pei used the session to signal Nothing's commercial ambitions in the US market. He announced that the company intends to take US phone sales seriously, with carrier partnership talks underway. He also confirmed new retail stores planned for New York City, Tokyo, London, and India in 2026, defining a flagship store as a showcase of a company's most creative ideas rather than simply a point of sale.

The US market context was significant: Nothing is already the largest audio products market, giving it a consumer base and brand recognition from which to launch a more aggressive phone push. The session therefore functioned simultaneously as a vision statement and a market entry signal.

## Extensive Media Coverage Amplified the Message

The session generated reporting from at least seven major outlets. TechCrunch published same-day coverage on March 18 headlined "Nothing CEO Carl Pei says smartphone apps will disappear as AI agents take their place." 9to5Google followed the next day with embedded video from the full session. Android Central ran coverage framing the story as Pei's belief that the app era is ending. PhoneArena, Tech Times, AI Tool Insight, and international outlets including Mezha.net and Business Tech Africa all covered the session. VML's SXSW 2026 trends report cited it as emblematic of the conference's broader theme around AI's practical impact on consumer technology.

## Conclusion

This session delivered a specific, testable thesis about the future of mobile computing rather than generalized AI speculation. The three-stage roadmap from command execution to autonomous agents, paired with the concrete argument that agents need purpose-built interfaces rather than screen-scraping workarounds, gave the predictions operational weight. The fact that these claims came from a CEO actively building toward that vision with $200 million in fresh capital, and simultaneously announcing US carrier talks and global retail expansion, meant the session carried commercial significance beyond its intellectual content.

For technology and marketing leaders tracking AI's real-world trajectory in consumer devices, Pei's framework offered the rarest commodity at SXSW: a founder's specific, time-bound, falsifiable prediction about what the next decade looks like.
