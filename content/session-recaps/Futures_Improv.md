# Futures Improv: Interactive Foresight and the Future of Boredom, BCIs, Climate, and Conspiracy

**Panelists:**
- Matt Carmichael (Ipsos, editor of "What the Future" magazine)
- Alexandra Whittington (Futurist, Future of Business team)
- Henry Coutinho Mason (Author of "The Future Normal" and "Future Words")
- Annie Hardy (Global AI Architect and Futurist, Cisco Systems)

**Date/Day:** Monday, March 16, 2026
**Format:** Interactive foresight session with audience-submitted topics, relay rounds, special guest segments, lightning rounds

## Overview

This second annual Foresight Improv session at South by Southwest brought together professional futurists to speculate on emerging futures through an experimental, interactive format. Rather than traditional panel discussions or lectures, the format uses improv techniques and real-time audience input to explore how different possible futures might unfold. Panelists respond to audience-voted topics in rapid relay rounds, engage special guest experts on niche topics, and present lightning-round perspectives on emerging concerns from work and UBI to brain-computer interfaces, climate change, and conspiracy theories. The session demonstrated how foresight—rigorously practiced but presented creatively—can illuminate possibilities while acknowledging uncertainty, and how the conversation about the future is fundamentally about the future we collectively choose to create.

## Key Themes and Insights

### The Future of Work and the Office

**Remote and Global Collaboration:** Work increasingly divorces from physical location and synchronous time. Global remote teams will create new forms of camaraderie and professional networks that transcend traditional office-based disciplines. The distinction between "architect," "teacher," and "actor" dissolves when these professions share remote workspaces and support each other across disciplinary boundaries.

**Office as Values Statement:** Rather than functional necessity, offices become active choices to express organizational values and serve as recruitment/retention tools. In a world of smaller, distributed teams, physical offices communicate commitment to culture and facilitate spontaneous cross-functional interaction. Annie Hardy noted that 3Space, her UK non-profit, converts empty commercial buildings into affordable workspaces, demonstrating that companies renting office space gain significant retention advantages by showing they support charitable causes.

**Entrepreneurial Future and Portfolio Careers:** The W2 employment relationship is ending, replaced by portfolio careers and gig economy structures. Workers must increasingly develop multiple income streams and creative approaches to earning rather than relying on single employers. This shift requires educational and cultural change, moving away from assumptions of linear career progression to embrace portfolio thinking.

**Agentic Work Redefinition:** As AI handles functional tasks, the distinction between work and culture becomes critical. If functions become automated, organizations must rethink whether physical presence serves the culture of work or the function of work. With agentic AI replacing functional tasks, the office must justify itself through community and cultural value rather than operational necessity.

**The Precarity Question:** While the gig economy is portrayed positively, it frequently represents people cobbling together income just to survive rather than by conscious choice. The distinction between entrepreneurial portfolio careers and forced precarity matters enormously for outcomes and wellbeing.

### Universal Basic Income (UBI)

**American Skepticism:** UBI seems antithetical to American mythology and assumptions about merit, work, and individual earning. The concept of giving people money to keep buying products made by AI companies creates a troubling cycle that contradicts fundamental American values around work and self-sufficiency.

**Geographic Variation:** UBI implementation will differ dramatically by region. Progressive European economies with robust taxation systems and human-centered policies are far more likely to adopt UBI first, particularly if it's understood as a different form of taxation for social benefit. Four U.S. states have already outlawed UBI, even when funded from private sources, indicating significant resistance.

**Social Status Concerns:** People are fundamentally driven by social status and desire to be better than one another. UBI struggles because it doesn't address this competitive drive—giving everyone the same income doesn't satisfy the human need for relative advantage and elevated status.

**Fully Automated Luxury Communism (FALC):** An alternative utopian vision proposes that if AI and robots handle all labor, basic human needs could be met at luxury levels—everyone with houses, food, and designer goods like Versace. This vision, explored in a book by an Italian author, represents a radically different endpoint from incremental UBI implementation.

**European Pathway:** The panelists predicted UBI will take 30-50 years to implement in wealthy economies due to required systemic economic restructuring. Europe's progressive taxation systems and human-centered policies position it as an earlier adopter than the United States, which faces ideological resistance to wealth redistribution.

### Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs)

**Medical Applications First:** BCIs currently impact people with special needs, paralysis, and quadriplegia, enabling brain-controlled device operation. Medical applications represent the ethical and practical first deployment, helping people regain lost function.

**Wealthy Early Adopters:** Like previous technologies (hallucinogens, micro-dosing), BCIs will first reach wealthy individuals wanting augmentation and competitive advantage. The parallel to Silicon Valley's early adoption of psychedelics for cognitive enhancement suggests similar patterns—technology available to elites seeking advantage before broader adoption.

**Neural Rights and Regulation:** The rise of BCIs necessitates focus on cerebral rights, neural security, and brain hacking protection—areas the FDA has barely addressed. Brain-computer interface governance involves social and human issues beyond technical ones, including who controls the chip, who manufactures it, and who governs its use.

**Geopolitical Risks:** Scenarios exist where global chip scarcity and intergovernment competition lead to weaponization of BCIs. In Amy Webb's thought experiment, China decides to disable all non-Chinese BCIs during resource scarcity, causing massive casualties. While speculative, this possibility highlights real governance gaps.

**Privacy in the Home:** BCIs in home environments raise profound privacy questions. Devices reading minds and transmitting information about household members create unprecedented surveillance risk. Companies controlling these systems could monitor family communications, parental authority, and intimate thoughts.

**Sustainability Challenges:** Companies providing invasive BCIs can fail, cease support, or force device removal, leaving users without alternatives. The ecosystem's sustainability and user protection require frameworks that don't yet exist.

### Climate Change

**Accurate Predictions:** Climate models from the 1970s have accurately predicted current conditions, with only errors in underestimating how quickly and severely impacts occur. Scientific accuracy about climate trajectory exists; the challenges are political and behavioral.

**Digital Twin Technology:** NVIDIA's digital twin of Earth enables real-time climate simulation at scale, supporting emergency response and long-term planning. This technology could help prevent climate emergencies through better predictive capacity.

**Non-Obvious Winners and Losers:** Climate change creates unexpected economic shifts. Champagne production is moving north from France as temperatures rise—creating both winners (English sparkling wine producers) and losers (French champagne industry facing existential pressure to rebrand).

**Optimistic Long-Term Scenario:** Over 100 years, declining birth rates, nuclear energy adoption, improved semiconductors, and material science advances could enable Earth's climate to heal. This optimistic pathway requires significant technological and social change but isn't impossible.

### Conspiracy Theories and Misinformation

**Origins in Scarcity:** Conspiracy theories historically stemmed from information scarcity—people lacked information and filled gaps with speculation. Now abundant information exists, but the driver of conspiracy theories has shifted to desire for human connection. People increasingly embrace conspiracy theories to connect with like-minded communities rather than from genuine lack of information.

**AI Amplification and Whack-a-Mole Dynamics:** Deep fakes, AI-generated voices, and spoofing make conspiracy theories more believable and harder to debunk. Simultaneously, AI detection tools improve at identifying misinformation—but as detection improves, misinformation creators learn from debunking. This creates a perpetual escalation where both finding and creating disinformation improve together.

**Trust Paradox:** Transparency is presented as the antithesis of trust—if you need transparency, trust doesn't exist. Yet distrust simultaneously drives transparency movements (blockchain, food movement transparency, supply chain visibility). This paradox means that even as distrust rises, it can produce positive outcomes (transparency, accountability) while also perpetuating conspiracy narratives.

**Distrust as Driver of Good:** While distrust is rising and conspiracy theories are worsening, distrust also drives positive innovations. Blockchain transparency, food movement transparency requirements, and supply chain visibility all emerged from distrust. The same dynamic enabling conspiracy theories also enables accountability mechanisms.

**Younger Generation Response:** Gen Beta and Gen Alpha will likely be more connected and less disconnected than Gen Z, potentially reducing conspiracy theory adoption. However, shorter attention spans and increased digital addiction raise concerns about misinformation susceptibility.

### The Future of Boredom

**Boredom's Creative Function:** Boredom is essential for creativity, shower thoughts, and innovative ideas. Current attention economy structures (energy drinks, notebooks, mindfulness apps, social media) attempt to eliminate boredom, paradoxically reducing the cognitive space where ideas emerge.

**Generational Attention Decline:** Generation Z has attention spans 50% shorter than millennials', with implications for Gen Alpha. This raises serious concerns about capacity for sustained thought and creative breakthrough.

**Cultural Shift Needed:** Creating space for healthy boredom requires rejecting dependency on attention-capturing systems and teaching children to embrace unstructured time. Current tech-saturated childhoods may produce generations unable to access the mental states that drive creativity.

**Boredom as Resistance:** In a future with UBI and AI-replaced jobs, boredom combined with free time could lead to conspiracy theory adoption or generative creativity depending on cultural responses. Celebrating unstructured stimulus (not doom-scrolling) rather than curated content is preferable but requires cultural change.

**Crime of Boredom:** In futures with significant free time, boredom combined with financial security could lead to antisocial behavior or worse—or it could produce extraordinary creativity. The outcome depends on cultural values and available alternatives.

### Special Guest Segments and Lightning Rounds

**Staycations (Michael Marshall):** The future of travel resembles glamping—premium, individualized experiences brought into homes rather than standardized hotels. Expect more personalized, local experiences rather than mass tourism.

**Lost Luggage (Michael Marshall):** Bags will have unique digital identities, flexible circuits, and computer vision enabling fingerprint-like identification without physical tags, eliminating loss through technology.

**Leadership (Diana Wu David):** In company-of-one models, everyone becomes their own leader. Intellectual property tokenizes on blockchain, generating royalties and replacing traditional organizational hierarchies.

**Currency (Diana Wu David):** Future currency may return to barter systems where assets like camels, goats, or South by Southwest badges become tradable mediums of exchange.

**Collaboration (Neil Redding):** Collaboration increasingly involves non-human AI agents working 24/7, with tension as their capabilities and stamina exceed human limits.

**Analog (Neil Redding):** The future of analog involves movement away from digital technology into naturally evolving ecosystems—like foraging for mushrooms in forests as alternative to digital consumption.

**Adolescent Rebellion (Lightning Round):** Teenage rebellion may become obsolete through CRISPR, eliminating negative aspects while preserving development—or remain a construct of industrial society potentially replaced.

**Protein (Lightning Round):** Cellular agriculture will produce locally-grown protein, potentially solving climate change through elimination of animal agriculture shipping and emissions.

**Fine Dining (Lightning Round):** Fine dining will democratize, becoming time and craft-based rather than cost-gated, part of bartering economy with abundant information access.

**Coaching (Lightning Round):** AI coaching for public speaking and athletic development will dramatically improve early development, democratizing coaching previously available only to wealthy youth.

**Urban Education (Lightning Round):** Cities will become learning environments where buildings, trees, and weather patterns provide real-time information and knowledge improvement.

**Conferences (Lightning Round):** Current conference models (traveling to sit and listen without creating together) will look "horrific" in three years. AI will enable surfacing insights from 300 people in 30 minutes versus three hours of passive listening.

**Women's Economic Role (Lightning Round):** With $83 trillion passing to women over the next decade (70% of generational wealth transfer), women will become foundational angel investors, ethical VC investors, and real estate investors, impacting not just domestic but investment economies.

## Key Frameworks and Concepts

**Foresight Practice:** Foresight differs from prediction because it acknowledges uncertainty while rigorously exploring possibilities. The panelists distinguished between rigorous academic foresight practice and the creative presentation that makes it accessible and engaging.

**Abundance vs. Scarcity Thinking:** Many future frameworks hinge on assumptions about resource scarcity or abundance. UBI, climate change, and even conspiracy theories reflect different abundance/scarcity assumptions that fundamentally reshape likely futures.

**Cultural Change as Prerequisite:** Technological possibility requires cultural receptivity. BCIs are technically feasible but face governance and cultural barriers. UBI is economically possible but faces cultural resistance rooted in values. Climate solutions exist but require political will.

**Multiplier Effects:** Technologies amplify human capability but also amplify human limitations. AI simultaneously improves disinformation detection and creation; the net effect depends on distribution and governance.

## Takeaways for Practitioners

1. **Embrace Portfolio Thinking:** Whether developing careers or business models, move away from dependence on single income streams or customer bases. Build resilience through diversification and multiple revenue streams.

2. **Distinguish Between Precarity and Choice:** When designing gig economy platforms or discussing future work, acknowledge that not all non-traditional employment is entrepreneurial. Support genuine choices rather than forced precarity.

3. **Plan for Regional Variation:** Assumptions that work globally often fail regionally. UBI, AI policy, BCIs, and climate responses will vary dramatically by geography, culture, and existing governance structures. Build regional flexibility into long-term plans.

4. **Invest in Rigorous Foresight:** Use foresight as practiced discipline (exploring possibilities systematically) rather than casual speculation. Combine rigor with creative presentation to make foresight accessible and actionable.

5. **Governance Precedes Widespread Adoption:** BCIs, AI, and other powerful technologies require governance frameworks BEFORE widespread adoption. Don't wait for problems—build frameworks proactively.

6. **Protect Psychological and Cognitive Space:** In an attention economy, protecting space for boredom, unstructured thinking, and digital-free time becomes a competitive advantage and creative necessity.

7. **Address Root Causes Over Symptoms:** Conspiracy theories aren't solved by debunking alone—they're solved by addressing the desire for human connection that drives them. Similarly, distrust isn't eliminated by transparency alone.

8. **Think in 30+ Year Timescales:** Significant systemic change (UBI implementation, climate stabilization, BCI governance) operates on 30-100 year timescales. Build patience and long-term commitment into strategic thinking.

9. **Watch for Non-Obvious Winners:** Climate change, technological disruption, and social transformation create unexpected winners and losers. Study second and third-order effects rather than obvious first-order impacts.

10. **Cultural Change is Prerequisite:** Technology is necessary but insufficient for large-scale change. Identify required cultural shifts and build support for them alongside technical innovation.

## Connection to SXSW 2026 Themes

Foresight Improv embodies several core SXSW themes:

**Creativity and Innovation:** By applying improvisational thinking to serious futures work, the session demonstrates how creative techniques (improv, real-time collaboration, surprise) can enhance rigorous analysis. This models the value of cross-disciplinary approaches.

**Audience Participation and Co-Creation:** Rather than delivering expert pronouncements, the session invited audience input (topic voting, questions, collaborative exploration). This reflects SXSW's emphasis on community participation in shaping narratives.

**Anticipation Over Reaction:** Foresight work is fundamentally about anticipating possible futures rather than reacting to present problems. For technologists, creators, and business leaders, this proactive stance is essential.

**Ethical Responsibility:** Multiple segments (BCIs, climate, conspiracy theories) emphasized the human and ethical dimensions of technological change. This aligns with SXSW's broader movement toward responsible innovation.

**Global Perspective with Local Variation:** Discussion of how UBI, BCIs, climate change, and other challenges manifest differently across geographies reflects SXSW's increasingly global perspective while respecting local contexts.

**Conversation Over Answers:** Rather than claiming to know what the future will be, the session modeled genuine inquiry and collaborative exploration. This uncertainty is appropriate for complex systems and real foresight work.
