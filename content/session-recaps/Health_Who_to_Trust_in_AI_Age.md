# Health: Who to Trust in the Age of AI

**Session Date:** Monday, March 16, 2026
**Format:** Panel Discussion with Q&A
**Moderator:** Mary James (Real Chemistry, Advanced Analytics)
**Panelists:** Alice Bell (Patient Advocate, Reddit Moderator), Dr. Anya Bilski (Emergency Medicine Physician, UCSF), Jessica Califf (Reddit Industry Director for Health), Dr. Anya Bilski (Moderator)

## Overview

This timely session examined the shifting landscape of health information in an era where AI, community platforms, and traditional healthcare intersect. The panel explored how patients increasingly turn to ChatGPT, Google, and community platforms like Reddit for health guidance before—or instead of—seeing a physician. Rather than positioning this as a threat, the discussion revealed opportunities for trust-building between patients, physicians, and AI tools when transparency, credibility assessment, and community moderation are prioritized.

The session highlighted the growing role of patient communities in healthcare decision-making, the strengths and limitations of AI-generated health information, and the critical importance of credibility verification across platforms. A common thread emerged: trust must be earned through transparency, whether from AI systems, human doctors, or online communities.

## Key Themes and Insights

### The Changing Health Information Journey

The health information journey has fundamentally shifted. Rather than a linear path to a doctor's appointment, patients now consult multiple sources—community platforms, AI tools, and traditional media—often in the middle of the night when immediate physician access is unavailable. This represents a significant change in how health anxiety is managed and addressed, with community platforms providing emotional support and information simultaneously.

The panel found that this multi-source approach is not necessarily negative. When patients arrive at appointments having researched their symptoms using AI or community feedback, they come better informed, ask more targeted questions, and engage more actively in their care decisions.

### Reddit as a Health Community Platform

Reddit has emerged as a major health information resource with:
- Over 20 years of health conversations with close to 1,000 health-specific communities
- Over 100,000 total communities with complete user anonymity
- 11.5 billion views to health content in the past year, up 65% year-over-year
- Communities moderated by passionate individuals who deeply care about their topics
- A "karma" system that incentivizes value contribution rather than engagement metrics

The anonymity and topic-based organization mean users surrounded by like-minded individuals experiencing similar conditions. This creates safe spaces for vulnerable conversations that people wouldn't have with friends or family, particularly around conditions they view as embarrassing or stigmatizing.

### Patient Advocacy and Community Moderation

Alice Bell's experience moderating the breast cancer subreddit for five years, combined with her own stage 3 breast cancer diagnosis, exemplifies how patient advocates become trusted voices. Her moderation work provided deep insights into how patients approach healthcare decisions and revealed that communities provide emotional support and practical guidance that doctors often cannot within time-limited appointments.

Key moderation responsibilities include:
- Clearing queues of posts and comments multiple times daily
- Responding to research requests and patient questions
- Adjusting rules based on community feedback
- Creating specialized sister communities to serve different patient needs
- Identifying urgent situations and directing members to immediate care

Communities actively identify when members are in crisis and collectively encourage them to seek emergency care, often successfully preventing serious health complications.

### Credibility Assessment and Trust Signals

Reddit maintains trust through several mechanisms:
- Specialized subreddits like r/askdocs require users to provide medical licenses as proof of expertise
- Community members actively verify credentials and flag outdated board certifications
- Moderators remove content from unverified individuals claiming medical authority
- Commercial interests and aligned incentives are scrutinized
- Scientists and doctors who create treatments become trusted voices and celebrities within health communities

This crowdsourced verification approach addresses the concern that unqualified practitioners might offer dangerous advice. The panel emphasized that trust requires transparency of sources, credentials, and potential conflicts of interest.

### OpenEvidence: AI Tools for Physicians

OpenEvidence represents the physician-side of AI-powered health information:
- Used by almost 50% of doctors in the United States
- Receives over 1 million questions per day from physicians
- Provides thoroughly cited answers linking directly to high-quality medical journals
- Has low hallucination risk because responses pull directly from publications rather than generating new content
- Growing globally to France, Canada, Brazil, and Australia

For clinical practice, OpenEvidence excels at clinical decision support, allowing doctors to give full attention to patients without documentation burden. Ambient scribing tools record and transcribe patient conversations directly into EMR systems like Epic, significantly reducing administrative load.

### Limitations and Biases of Consumer AI

Recent studies reveal important limitations of consumer AI for health:
- AI tends to under-diagnose real medical emergencies and over-diagnose when patients are actually fine
- Performance is strong with mid-acuity cases but struggles at clinical extremes
- Can contribute to "cyberchondria" where patients worry about rare conditions instead of common explanations
- Performs better when users provide granular details and clear clinical information

Patients should understand that AI is a tool for information retrieval, not a replacement for clinical training and pattern recognition developed over years of medical practice.

### Emotional Drivers of Misinformation

Misinformation spreads not because of information quality but because of emotional resonance. Communities can address emotions driving people toward unproven treatments—like fear motivating people toward hyperbaric oxygen therapy instead of proven chemotherapy—by validating the emotional reality while providing alternative frameworks.

### Physician Perspectives on AI

Dr. Bilski welcomes informed patients who research using ChatGPT or Claude before appointments because:
- Better research empowers patients to ask better questions
- More detailed information helps with diagnostic detective work
- Improved patient information supports better clinical decision-making
- The physician role becomes more about the "human last mile"—difficult conversations, goals of care discussions, and diagnosis disclosure

However, physicians must maintain concern about over-reliance on AI. Clinical gestalt—the gut feeling developed from years of seeing patterns—remains crucial and cannot be fully replicated by tools trained on published data.

### The Future of Healthcare with AI

The consensus emerged that AI will not replace physicians but will enhance their capabilities:
- Doctors must stay in the "pilot seat" and maintain clinical judgment
- AI helps physicians practice better overall through supportive tools
- Human doctors will flourish in difficult conversations, goals of care discussions, and diagnosis disclosure
- The partnership leverages AI's strengths in information retrieval with human judgment, empathy, and pattern recognition

### Community Growth and Unmet Needs

Women's health conditions are seeing significant growth:
- Endometriosis subreddit has over 230,000 weekly visitors
- These are often understudied conditions with high rates of patient misunderstanding
- Communities showcase unmet needs that marketers should address
- Growth spans caregivers, patients, and healthcare professionals

### The Importance of Patient Education

All stakeholders must become educated consumers and educators:
- Communities maintain trust through consistent foundation of self-correcting communities
- Local moderation and anonymity are essential
- Marketers should read community conversations to understand actual patient needs
- Authentic participation requires providing value through long-form, detailed content addressing community questions

## Takeaways for Practitioners

**For Healthcare Organizations:** Patient communities are not competitors but valuable sources of unmet patient needs. Organizations that listen to these communities and provide responsive, transparent information will build stronger patient relationships. The panel emphasized that 11.5 billion views to health content represents genuine demand that healthcare can better serve.

**For Physicians:** Patients using AI tools for research before appointments are actually a positive development. These patients are more engaged, better informed, and provide more detailed clinical information. Physicians should welcome this preparation while maintaining their role in clinical judgment and the human dimensions of care that AI cannot replicate.

**For Tech Companies:** AI tools for health must prioritize transparency of sources and quality of evidence. The success of OpenEvidence demonstrates that physicians trust AI when responses are thoroughly cited and linked to high-quality sources. Consumer AI tools should adopt similar standards rather than generating new content.

**For Marketers and Brands:** The 230,000 weekly visitors to the endometriosis subreddit and similar communities represent real marketing opportunities. However, authentic participation requires providing genuine value, understanding community norms, and being transparent about commercial interests. Reading community conversations is the first step toward understanding what real patients actually need.

**For Patients:** Use multiple information sources, but verify credentials and bias. Understand that AI is excellent for information retrieval but not a replacement for clinical training. Come to appointments with questions rather than self-diagnoses, and use community support for emotional validation alongside professional care.

**For Platform Designers:** Community moderation, anonymity, and topic-based organization are essential for building trust in health communities. The karma system and volunteer moderation model have proven more effective than algorithmic feeds for health conversations.

## Connection to SXSW 2026 Themes

This session directly addresses SXSW 2026's core theme of human connection and authenticity in the age of AI. The discussion reveals that as AI tools proliferate, the human dimensions of healthcare—emotional support, physical presence, clinical judgment developed through years of experience—become even more valuable, not less.

The panel demonstrates that authenticity matters. AI-generated health information without transparent sourcing, physicians without empathy, and online communities without genuine moderation all fail to build trust. Conversely, transparent AI, human doctors who listen, and volunteer-moderated communities that genuinely serve members all succeed.

The session also highlights how technology should augment human connection rather than replace it. Reddit's health communities work because they connect humans with shared experiences. OpenEvidence works for physicians because it supports rather than replaces clinical judgment. The future of healthcare, the panel suggests, is not AI versus humans but rather AI and humans working together, each contributing their unique strengths.

This reflects the festival's broader exploration of what remains essential about human experience in an increasingly technological world.
