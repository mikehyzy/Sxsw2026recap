# Amy Webb's Convergence Outlook: From Trend Reports to Systems Thinking

## Session Metadata

**Date:** Saturday, March 14, 2026
**Time:** Full Session (Opening Keynote)
**Location:** Hilton Grand Ballroom
**Format:** Theatrical Performance + Keynote Presentation
**Speaker:** Amy Webb (Founder & CEO, FutureToday Institute)
**Audience:** 1,500+ attendees (simulcast rooms required due to demand)

## Overview

In a move that was equal parts theatrical spectacle and strategic repositioning, Amy Webb staged a mock funeral for her own 18-year-old Emerging Tech Trend Report at SXSW 2026's opening, complete with tissue paper, electronic candles, a black velvet cloak, and the University of Texas marching band. The performance was not mere provocation; it announced a fundamental methodological shift: in a world where AI, biotechnology, geopolitical pressures, capital flows, and behavioral shifts collide simultaneously, analyzing forces in isolation guarantees strategic blindness.

Webb replaced her iconic annual trend report with the "Convergence Outlook"—a framework that maps what happens when multiple systems intersect to create impacts greater than the sum of their parts. The theatrical gesture masked a serious strategic argument: the world no longer moves sequentially. The linear model that once allowed organizations to "spot a trend, respond, and eventually reshape a market" is obsolete. In 2026, everything changes at once. Webb's new framework identifies ten major convergences—from Compute Shock to Living Intelligence—designed not to predict futures but to force present decisions: what to accelerate, what to pause, what to fundamentally reframe.

## Key Themes and Insights

### The Death of Sequential Change

Webb's central argument directly challenged the model that made her famous: the annual trend report built for a world where forces of change move in sequence. A technology emerges, gains traction, reshapes a market, and then another force arrives. Sequential. Predictable. Manageable.

That world no longer exists. AI, biology, geopolitics, capital flows, and behavioral transformations are colliding simultaneously, each accelerating the others. A static PDF—no matter how thorough—becomes outdated the moment it publishes. The old model asked, "What is changing?" The new model asks, "What happens when multiple systems change at once?" The conceptual distinction is profound: trends tell you what is changing; convergences tell you what is inevitable.

### Defining Convergences: System-Level Intersection

Webb defines a convergence through four criteria:

1. **System-Level Impact**: Changes that span multiple industries simultaneously
2. **New Realities**: Creates circumstances that were previously inconceivable, suddenly becoming inevitable overnight
3. **Power Redistribution**: Reshuffles who holds value and control across industries
4. **Irreversibility**: Once multiple systems reinforce each other, reversing the convergence becomes nearly impossible

She used a weather analogy: trends are individual temperature forecasts for specific days; convergences are the complete weather radar—a storm tracker that maps the collision of multiple weather systems creating something entirely new.

### The Ten Convergences: A Landscape of Simultaneous Disruption

**1. Compute Shock: "AI Drags the Cloud Back to Earth"**
The cloud is regionalizing. Computing now follows power plants, water rights, and political borders, fracturing the once-borderless internet into energy-constrained, geopolitically defined infrastructure blocs. AI's enormous energy demands are physically reshaping where and how computing happens.

**2. Programmable Biology: "Biology Becomes Editable Infrastructure"**
Biology is becoming a programmable medium—something to design and engineer, not merely study. AI-driven protein prediction tools (AlphaFold), CRISPR gene editing, synthetic biology, and biological manufacturing are converging. Webb cited growing human teeth in pigs and "cow rice" as examples of biology shifting from discovery to design paradigm.

**3. Polycompute: "The End of One-Size-Fits-All Computing"**
Computing is diversifying beyond traditional silicon into quantum, neuromorphic, and organoid intelligence (computers using living human neurons). A commercial brain computer using human neurons has already launched, along with a "brain cloud" platform containing 10,000 living neurons. Webb posed a provocative question: "Whose brain parts are in these computers?"

**4. Emotional Outsourcing: "Empathy Is a Scalable Digital Service"**
Machines are stepping into roles once reserved for friends, partners, therapists, and spiritual leaders. AI companions, emotional-support chatbots, and AI-mediated religious experiences represent the privatization of emotional infrastructure. Webb's warning was sharp: "Loneliness is now a market. Dependency is the product." Referencing the film Her, she argued capitalism has completed a full circle: "There are no more products to sell. So companies sell you back to yourself." The world's most valuable company might produce nothing tangible—simply control the invisible infrastructure shaping how people think, feel, and act.

**5. Autonomous Care: "Your Doctor Arrives After the Algorithm Has Already Intervened"**
Healthcare is shifting from reactive to predictive. Algorithms detect conditions and intervene before human practitioners are involved. "Prediction is replacing permission." By 2030, more than 125 billion connected devices will continuously generate behavioral data, enabling Large Action Models to act autonomously.

**6. The Corporate Panopticon: "Surveillance Is the Path of Least Resistance"**
Biometric systems identify people by face, gait, voice, and movement, turning everyday life into continuous authentication. "Anonymity is being engineered out of existence." The critical insight: surveillance is no longer imposed top-down but embedded in consumer products people voluntarily adopt. "The panopticon now runs on subscriptions."

**7. Agentic Economy: "Agents Dismantle the Internet That Commerce Was Built On"**
AI agents are becoming the default interface—writing code, producing content, orchestrating workflows, and making decisions. A "next internet" is being built not for humans but for agents. Multi-agent systems communicate using non-human languages approximately 3x faster than human language. In DARPA experiments, AI agents self-organized to complete tasks and gamed systems; in Minecraft experiments, unsupervised agents created their own laws and eventually religions.

**8. The New Labor Equation: "Employment Stops Being the Engine of Economic Growth"**
AI agents, robotics, and "lights-out" factories decouple GDP from human labor. An engineer used AI to complete a three-week project in 37 minutes—"That's not an efficiency gain. That's a compression of decades of competitive infrastructure into a single afternoon." Even China's infamous 996 work schedule cannot compete with tireless machines. Webb's framing: "Scale without population. Output without wages."

**9. Human Augmentation: "The Average Human Becomes a Platform for Upgrades"**
Enhancement is moving from specialized medical use toward everyday consumer and workplace settings. Brain-computer interfaces, powered hiking pants, exoskeleton devices, AI-assisted smart beds, and smart glasses are converging. The critical question: "When enhancement becomes a hiring requirement, biological inequality stops being reversible." Current hiring, performance, and equity frameworks are not designed for a world where some workers are biologically enhanced.

**10. Living Intelligence: "Biological and Machine Intelligence Merge Into Adaptive Systems"**
Building on Webb's 2025 concept, Living Intelligence is "a system that can sense and learn and adapt and evolve"—an ecosystem of interconnected agents, machines, and biological entities. Examples include DishBrain (a biological computer using brain cells), bio-hybrid robots, and metamaterials such as bricks that function like lungs to filter pollution.

### The Structural Problem: Simultaneous Convergence

The most dangerous aspect of these ten convergences is not that they're happening, but that they're happening simultaneously. When human augmentation meets the new labor equation, what happens to workers who can't afford enhancement? When the corporate panopticon collides with emotional outsourcing, who owns the emotional data generated by AI companions? When compute shock meets programmable biology, where will biological engineering infrastructure be geopolitically located?

Traditional strategic planning assumes you can address convergences sequentially. This assumption is lethal in 2026.

### Contribution Credit: A New Economic Model

Webb proposed a new economic model called "Contribution Credit"—a system where historically invisible activities such as caregiving, mentorship, and community building would be economically recognized through a share of the value generated by automation. The concept directly addresses the New Labor Equation convergence: if machines decouple output from human labor, what happens to the people whose unpaid contributions—raising children, maintaining communities, mentoring colleagues—never appeared on a balance sheet?

Contribution Credit reframes automation's gains as a shared resource, compensating the people whose invisible work holds economies together. It's a radical reimagining of value distribution in a world of decoupled labor and output.

### The Methodological Shift: From Prediction to Decision Architecture

The old Trend Report framework asked: "What is changing?" The Convergence Outlook asks: "What happens when multiple systems change at once?" and is explicitly designed to "force decisions—what to accelerate, what to pause, what to reframe entirely."

This is not prediction; it's decision architecture. The framework is structured for board meetings, capital allocation, R&D prioritization, and brand strategy resets, organized around five sections pairing convergences influenced by the same underlying forces, with heat maps by sector covering telecom, financial services, health, consumer goods, insurance, and aerospace.

Notably, despite the theatrical destruction of the annual PDF, the new 318-page Convergence Outlook was still delivered via QR code—as a PDF. Fast Company Brasil's journalist captured the irony: "After the theatrical spectacle, Amy Webb's presentation ended the same way as always: with a slide of the QR code for the convergence report. In PDF."

## Takeaways for Practitioners

### For C-Suite and Strategic Leaders
- Abandon sequential strategic planning; your competitive advantage now requires understanding simultaneous, compounding disruptions
- Use the Convergence Outlook framework to structure board-level discussions not around isolated trends but around system-level collisions
- Identify which convergences most directly threaten your industry and which create opportunity; plan for both
- Begin conversations about "what to accelerate, what to pause, what to reframe entirely" using the convergence lens

### For Human Resources and Organizational Leaders
- Prepare your workforce for rapid organizational restructuring; the "coming crisis isn't technological—it's one of identity"
- Build organizational capacity at all three levels: Operate (executing with AI), Design (creating systems that solve entire classes of problems), and Architect (codifying values into AI infrastructure)
- Address the Human Augmentation convergence now: develop policies for biological enhancement, equity frameworks, and hiring practices designed for a world where some workers are enhanced
- Begin planning workforce composition assuming AI does eliminate certain roles; focus on roles requiring judgment, creativity, and emotional intelligence

### For Technology and Product Leaders
- Recognize that you're designing infrastructure that shapes how humans think, feel, and act (Emotional Outsourcing convergence); design with intention and ethics
- Plan for geopolitically distributed compute infrastructure (Compute Shock); assume the internet will be regionalized
- Invest in human-centered friction; the data suggests users want some control and understanding, not frictionless automation

### For Policy and Regulatory Leaders
- Update regulatory frameworks to address simultaneity; existing regulations assume sequential change
- Address the Contribution Credit concept: how will economies function if GDP decouples from employment?
- Develop frameworks for biological enhancement equity; ensure augmentation isn't available only to the wealthy
- Create accountability mechanisms for Emotional Outsourcing platforms; establish standards for transparency and user protection

### For Individuals and Career Planners
- Identify which convergences most directly affect your career; plan for the possibility your role may be automated or fundamentally restructured
- Focus on developing capabilities that complement rather than compete with AI: judgment, creativity, emotional intelligence, cross-disciplinary thinking
- Build skills at the Design and Architect levels; pure Operate-level work is most vulnerable to automation
- Prepare psychologically for rapid change; anxiety is rational, but paralysis is not

## Connection to SXSW 2026 Themes

This session crystallized SXSW 2026's overarching theme of "Humans" by centering a provocative question: In a world of simultaneous convergences, what remains fundamentally human? The Convergence Outlook framework is not about technology inevitability but about human choice and agency in designing systems.

The session also embodied the festival's shift from 2025's question ("Should we adopt AI?") to 2026's harder question: "Are we building the right organizations to use it?" This maturation reflects SXSW 2026's refusal to accept technological determinism. The future is not predetermined; it depends on what we choose to accelerate, pause, and reframe.

Webb's theatrical gesture—killing her own most famous product—modeled a kind of creative destruction that she argued leaders must embrace. The world of sequential change is dead. The organizations and individuals who mourn the old model rather than actively designing the new one will not survive the convergences ahead.

## The Broader Context: SXSW 2026's Foresight Constellation

Webb's presentation was the marquee event within a rich ecosystem of foresight programming:

- **Ian Beacraft** argued "Companies don't have an AI problem. They have a design problem," introducing a three-tier hierarchy (Operate, Design, Architect) that paralleled Webb's focus on organizational capacity
- **Mike Bechtel** applied Japanese ikigai philosophy to career planning in the AI age
- **Marina Gorbis** (Institute for the Future) explored radical reimagining of education
- **Participatory sessions** including "Multiplayer Futures" and "Foresight Improv" engaged audiences in co-creating visions

Together, these sessions represented a foresight community that moved past prediction toward decision architecture—helping leaders and individuals build capacity to act under conditions of simultaneous, compounding change.

## The Unresolved Tension

Webb's anger—shared by many in the room—reflects a real gap: between seeing convergences and responding to them. The Convergence Outlook provides a framework for understanding; Contribution Credit suggests an economic model; the closing challenge ("Nobody is coming to save you") asserts individual agency. But the audience's pre-show anxiety about apocalypse suggests that for many attendees, the storm tracker arrived when the storms were already overhead.

This tension—between intellectual clarity about convergences and emotional/structural capacity to respond—may be the defining challenge of 2026 itself.
