# Food and Comics: Culinary Storytelling and Creative Activism

**Session Date:** Tuesday, March 17, 2026
**Format:** Panel Discussion
**Speakers:** Chef Jose Andres (World Central Kitchen), Steve Orlando (Comic Writer), Nick Lowe (Marvel Editor)

## Overview

This SXSW panel brought together two of humanity's most universal languages—food and art—to explore how culinary activism and comic storytelling can drive social change. Chef Jose Andres, celebrated restaurateur and humanitarian, joined writer Steve Orlando and Marvel editor Nick Lowe to discuss the intersection of food-focused narratives and comic book storytelling. The conversation spanned from the historical significance of these mediums to concrete examples of how major publishers are leveraging comics to amplify messages of compassion and community resilience in crisis zones around the world.

The session highlighted the synergy between culinary expertise and visual storytelling, demonstrating that whether through a plate of food or a comic book panel, powerful narratives can inspire action. Andres' World Central Kitchen work, which has served nearly one billion meals in disaster zones, provided real-world context for how individual talent and determination can scale to meet humanity's greatest challenges.

## Key Themes and Insights

### The Power of Universal Languages
Food and art serve as two of humanity's most universal languages. The panel emphasized that through food-focused comics, something magical happens when chefs enter the frame—the storytelling transcends typical entertainment to become a vehicle for social messages about human dignity and care in times of crisis.

### Everyday Superheroes and Personal Dignity
World Central Kitchen volunteers are recognized as everyday superheroes working in conflict zones like Gaza and Ukraine. The panel stressed that these are not mythical characters—they are real people maintaining personal dignity and providing immediate help to those in crisis, even when larger systems fail. Spider-Man emerged as the perfect symbolic character because, like Jose himself, he represents helping people where they are with what you have, focusing on personal dignity even in the midst of chaos.

### From Twitter Friendship to Multimedia Collaborations
The panel traced how a chance interaction on Twitter between Jose and Steve Orlando in 2013, initially about "food porn," evolved into major collaborative projects. This underscored the power of one-on-one human connection in driving creative partnerships. What began as a passing interaction eventually led to the graphic novel "Feeding Dangerously," a five-year project documenting World Central Kitchen's disaster relief work across Puerto Rico, Guatemala, the Bahamas, and beyond.

### Using Comics as Activism
The conversation revealed how comics serve as a medium to inspire people to use their talents in service of others. The panel discussed various food-focused comics from Marvel and DC, including "Get Jiro," "Hungry Ghost," and the newly released "Spider-Man: Meals to Astonish," which integrate culinary narratives with superhero storytelling to reach passionate fan bases with messages of compassion.

### Scaling Impact Through Multiple Mediums
Marvel's recent announcements—including "Spider-Man: Meals to Astonish" and the October 2026 release of "Avengers: Meals to Astonish"—demonstrate how major publishers are embedding World Central Kitchen's mission into their storytelling. These projects tie directly to the Avengers Emergency Response Squad concept, which exists because of World Central Kitchen's pioneering work in disaster response.

### The Documentary Approach
"Feeding Dangerously" stands out as a documentary graphic novel that meticulously recreated real moments from disaster zones. With over 250 pieces of artwork and collaboration from artist Alberto Conticelli, the project captured the authentic experiences of families in volcanic regions, tsunami areas, and war zones. This approach elevated the comic format beyond entertainment to become historical documentation.

### Building Community Beyond Transactions
The panel stressed the importance of reciprocal relationships and genuine community engagement. Jose emphasized that World Central Kitchen started with just $100,000 in 2010 and has grown to serve nearly a billion meals because of genuine human connection and the understanding that everyone has talents to contribute. The focus is on building community, not just transactions.

## Takeaways for Practitioners

**For Creative Professionals:** The collaboration between Jose, Steve, and Alberto demonstrates the power of one-on-one engagement in building meaningful creative partnerships. Rather than waiting for perfect opportunities, consistent, authentic communication can lead to extraordinary collaborative outcomes.

**For Nonprofits and Activists:** Using existing cultural properties and entertainment mediums to amplify your message is significantly more effective than expecting audiences to seek out your work. Major publishers' willingness to integrate World Central Kitchen's mission into their storytelling shows how organizations can reach passionate audiences through entertainment that respects both the message and the medium.

**For Business Leaders:** The transformation of a $100,000 initial investment into a billion-meal operation demonstrates the power of mission-driven leadership and community engagement. Jose's willingness to involve collaborators genuinely and to recognize volunteers as heroes—not just labor—created sustainable growth.

**For Storytellers:** Food and comics are powerful narrative tools because they are universal—everyone eats, and visual storytelling transcends language barriers. Using these mediums to tell stories about human dignity and compassion makes the stories more resonant than abstract advocacy.

**For Community Organizations:** The panel emphasized that it's crucial to celebrate and recognize everyday heroes. Making space for volunteers to see themselves in the larger mission—through comics, media, and public recognition—drives deeper engagement and commitment.

## Connection to SXSW 2026 Themes

This session exemplifies SXSW's core themes around human connection and authenticity in the age of AI. While technology and AI were not the primary focus, the panel's emphasis on one-on-one relationships, authentic storytelling, and the irreplaceable human elements of care and compassion speak to the festival's broader exploration of what makes us human.

The session also addresses the intersection of art and impact—how creative industries can be forces for social good. In an era of technological disruption, the panel demonstrates that human creativity, culinary expertise, and visual storytelling remain essential for building empathy and driving real-world change. The use of mainstream comics to normalize and celebrate humanitarian work shows how culture and activism can converge to shift collective values.
